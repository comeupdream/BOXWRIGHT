The questions that decide the budget

Why is my per-kit price higher than the item prices add up to?

Three things sit on top of goods: decoration (a per-unit rate plus a one-time setup for each screen, die or mold), assembly labour at $2.65–$4.50 per kit depending on volume, and packaging plus freight. The quote panel breaks out every one of them, and the setup fees are the reason a 25-kit run costs far more per kit than a 500-kit run of the same assortment.

What actually drives the lead time?

The slowest item in the box. A kit with an embroidered varsity jacket (35 production days) ships on the jacket's schedule no matter how fast the stickers are. Add five assembly days — eight over 500 kits — and that's your date. Rush cuts production roughly in half, with an eight-day floor we will not promise past.

Do quantity breaks apply per item or across the order?

Per item, on total units of that line — 100 kits with two pens each is 200 pens and prices at the 100+ break. Decoration stays flat per unit because a screen prints at the same speed either way. Separately, orders over $10,000 in goods earn a 3–7% program discount on the merchandise subtotal.

Can you ship to each recipient individually?

Yes. Choose individual drop-ship and we'll send a link that collects addresses, sizes and dietary notes, then ship each kit with its own tracking. Domestic runs about $9.40 a kit; mixed-international is estimated at $26 with duties quoted separately once the destination list is in.

What about apparel sizing?

Size collection happens on the same recipient form. For bulk-to-one-address runs we default to a standard curve (S 10% / M 25% / L 30% / XL 22% / 2XL 10% / 3XL 3%) and you can override it. Extended sizes above 2XL carry a small upcharge that we quote explicitly rather than burying.

Is there a minimum?

Twelve kits, and each individual item carries its own factory minimum — soft-enamel pins start at 100 units, knit socks at 50, most apparel at 12–24. The builder flags any line that falls under, rather than quoting a number a factory would refuse.
